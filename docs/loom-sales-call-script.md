# CraftHub sales-call Loom — target: 4:30–5:00

This is a call, not a product tour. Speak naturally; use this as a spine, not a script to read aloud.

## 0:00–0:35 — Open with the operational pain

“Thanks for taking the time. I’m looking at one narrow problem in SHK service firms: when a customer has a heating issue or asks about a replacement, the office has to clarify the same details, find the right information, and get it to the right technician—often while the phone is ringing. Before I show anything: where does that usually get stuck in your business?”

Pause. If this is a recording, state the assumed answer honestly: “For this demo, I’ll assume the office spends time clarifying and routing these requests manually.”

## 0:35–1:20 — Qualify in their numbers

“Roughly how many enquiries like that reach you on a busy day? And how many minutes does the office spend before a technician gets a usable request? If we removed just 30 minutes a day of that back-and-forth, would that matter more as faster response for customers, or more office capacity?”

“I’m not proposing you replace your existing software. The question is whether an AI layer in front of it earns its place by removing the repetitive work.”

## 1:20–2:45 — Demonstrate the grounded knowledge answer

Show the Chat screen. Ask: **“What should a homeowner check before planning a heating replacement?”**

“This answer is not a generic chatbot response. It is retrieved from an approved PDF, and the staff member can see the source document, page and supporting excerpt. In production these would be your installation manuals, service policies, supplier documents and past case guidance—not public demo material.”

Point to the citation. “That matters because a helpful-looking answer without a source is a liability risk; a response with evidence can be checked and escalated.”

## 2:45–3:40 — Show the workflow output

Show the Intake screen. Enter a believable customer example: heating not starting, address, preferred appointment. Submit it.

“Now the same conversation becomes a structured request: contact details, issue, address and preferred date—not a vague email or a note someone has to interpret. The next step is a follow-up draft with proposed slots, ready for your current process.”

## 3:40–4:30 — Handle objections before they are voiced

“You might say: ‘Our documents are inconsistent, and we already have software.’ That’s exactly why I would not start with a big rollout. We would start with 10–20 documents and one enquiry type. You retain the system of record; we measure whether the office spends less time clarifying and whether answers are trusted because they are cited.”

## 4:30–5:00 — Close concretely

“Would you be open to a two-week design-partner pilot? We would map one intake flow, load a small approved knowledge pack, and agree one measure—such as time from enquiry to technician-ready request. If it doesn’t save time or improve response quality, we stop. Can we book 30 minutes next week to choose that workflow and the first documents?”
